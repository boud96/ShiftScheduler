# Shift Scheduler, Phase 1.1: decisions and open items

These notes keep what the Phase 1.1 build decided on the user's behalf, and what it left for later. They come from the build's working ledger, which was not kept in git. Where a ruling differs from the plan (`2026-11-08-shift-scheduler-phase-1-1.md`) or the spec (`../specs/2026-11-07-shift-scheduler-phase-1-1-design.md`), the ruling describes what was built. The rulings of Phase 1 are in `2026-11-04-shift-scheduler-phase-1-notes.md`; the two numberings are separate, so "R7" here is not R7 there.

Some of the rulings go beyond the spec or change the plan:
- R3: the running counts of fairness take in only the months the app planned (those with a record), not the days entered as context.
- R7: the weekly rest (M11) also reads the week before the 7 days of the tail.
- R8: STRONG is 3e6, not the plan's 2e6.
- R24 to R30: the generator repairs the hours that the annealing leaves short (Task 6b), a task the plan did not have.
- R31: the weekend minimum (M12) gives way to a person's own maximum of 12-hour shifts.
- R32: a save file with a doctor's note of a person who is not in the team opens, with the note dropped.

## Rulings (R1–R38)

Each ruling says what was decided, why, and what it costs if the decision was wrong.

- R1: Between Tasks 3 and 6 the generator scenarios (`scenarios.test.ts`) may fail because of the new rules. Tasks 3 to 5 report the failing scenarios and seeds and change no assertion and no seed. Task 6 turns them green, and changes an expectation only where it contradicts the new spec (for example, the impossible month now also yields M12). Why: spec §6 puts generator quality in one place. Cost if wrong: the suite was red for a few tasks, and reviewers had to read failures as expected.
- R2: The parser keeps refusing a doctor note whose person is not in `people`, as the plan says, and `removePerson` deletes the person's notes (with a test that a plan saved after the removal still opens). Why: no path other than `removePerson` can make such a note. Cost if wrong: an orphan note from an unforeseen path makes a file unopenable until it is edited by hand. R32 changed this.
- R3: The history of the running counts takes in only earlier months, within the fairness window, that have a record in `data.months` (the app planned them: generated as a draft, or closed). Days entered only as the tail of a month do not count, and the window is clamped to 0–24 months. The Q1 scenario creates a month record when it applies a generated month, as `applyGenerated` does. Why: the tail is partial context and is most likely in the starting counts already, so counting it counts twice and skews the first month; spec §3.7 says the starting counts cover what came before the first month. Cost if wrong: a month filled in entirely by hand, without `Vygenerovat` (Generate), does not count toward fairness.
- R4: The group of starting counts on the `Tým` (Team) card is titled `Počáteční počty (před prvním naplánovaným měsícem)` (Starting counts, before the first planned month), not `… před prvním měsícem v souboru` (before the first month in the file); its test is named after it. Why: under R3 the boundary is the first planned month, not any month that holds cells. Cost if wrong: one label.
- R5: The test "brings everyone up to their FPD where it can" in `search.test.ts` falls under R1 as well. Task 6 owns generator quality, may change `construct.ts` as well as `moves.ts`, and must turn both test files green without weakening an assertion or re-seeding. Why: the new hard musts are what the spec asks for, and a review experiment found them feasible at 300,000 iterations, so the fix is in the search and not in the rules. Cost if wrong: if the search could not recover, the rules or their penalty shapes would have to be revisited.
- R6: W3's length part charges (length − 5) × W3 for a run of more than 5 days up to `settings.maxDaysInRow`; a run over the maximum gets M9 only. This amends the brief's "a run of exactly 6 costs W3". Why: at the default maximum of 6 it is the brief's behaviour, and the design's "ideally at most 5" then holds at any setting the `Pravidla` (Rules) screen allows (4–7). Cost if wrong: one W3 shape differs from the plan's text at a maximum other than 6.
- R7: `EnginePerson` gains `restBefore: number | null`: when the person's last shift in the 7 days before the tail ended, in minutes from midnight of the first day of the row (0 or less, or more for a night that ends on that day), read from the stored cells; null when no shift is stored there. The weekly rest starts the rest before the first shift of the row there and no longer takes it as unknown. This amends the plan's "the time before the first known shift counts as rest". Why: otherwise February's generator can make an M11 that only January's `Kontrola` (Checks) shows (for example an N12 on Sun 24 January, 25 January free, an R1 on Tue 26 January: 24 h of rest, and February's window from 26 January passes on an assumed long rest). Cost if wrong: one more context field and a look-back of 7 more days in `buildMonthGrid`.
- R8: STRONG is 3e6, not the plan's 2e6, with a test that STRONG > WEEKEND_SHORT + 11.5 × BELOW_FPD. It stays below GAP (1e7). Why: one N12 can earn both gains of the tier below at once (1.15e6 + 1e6 = 2.15e6 > 2e6), so a person who never takes nights and is short of FPD and of the weekend minimum would be given a night that someone else could cover, against spec §3.3 and §3.8. Cost if wrong: none expected; a rare inversion of tiers would stay.
- R9: W5b's k-th weekend against a person's style costs k² × W5b (1, then 4, then 9 times; 1, 5 and 14 cumulatively). Why: spec §3.8 leaves the penalty values to the builder; the shape matches the team lead's "meaningful time off, ideally two days, not scattered" and Phase 1's W5; on `jan2027` it passed 96 of 96 seeds against 88 with a linear cost. Cost if wrong: doctor's notes are honoured less often (73 of 96 against 87 of 96 with a linear cost); a change of one function in `weekendWishes.ts`.
- R10: A drop of 41 % in iterations per second is accepted. Why: the app spends a time budget, not a number of iterations, and at equal time the new search is far better (at 2 s, the seeds that pass: `jan2027` 0 to 22, `dec2027` 7 to 23, `impossible` 7 to 24; the month of the end-to-end spec at 5 s scores 2423 against 2592, with `Musí: 0` on all). Cost if wrong: a slow laptop gets fewer iterations, though still more per second than before.
- R11: The `doctor` scenario passes 19 of 24 seeds (73 of 96 over 1–96), and the one redesign of its notes is accepted. Why: the brief left the notes open; the redesign gives every note a shift the person does not dislike, so the test measures the search (W10 on: 73 of 96; off: 32 of 96) and not W10 losing to W2 or W6 by design. The configured seed passes, and nothing was re-seeded. Cost if wrong: more collisions with appointments, which the `L` marker shows and the team lead fixes by hand.
- R12: `q1-2027` keeps the brief's "within 2" for the 12-hour shifts of the quarter. The pull of W7 across the quarter (a spread of 2 on 17 of 24 seeds, of 1 on 7) is a tuning question for the team lead. Why: spec §3.7 asks for the running counts to feed W7, which the per-month assertion on the history checks, and W7's level can be changed on the `Pravidla` screen. Cost if wrong: some people get one 12-hour shift more than others over a quarter.
- R13: The 12h figure in the totals shows the counted shifts plus `+N` for N claimed holiday shifts (for example `3+1`), with a title that says the extra ones are claimed holidays, and turns red when the person is below the weekend minimum as M12 judges it. Why: spec §3.4 counts a claimed shift toward neither limit and the team lead's words were "+1 dvanáctka" (one more 12-hour shift); a bare 4 would read as M5 broken while `Kontrola` lists nothing; M12 names no day, so, like M6, the totals are where it shows. Cost if wrong: one figure's format and one class, in `Grid.tsx` and its test.
- R14: `Vymazat` (Clear) clears the cell's code and keeps its doctor's note; the note goes only with `Odebrat lékaře` (Remove doctor). Why: a note records an appointment, which stays true whatever the cell holds, and clearing a shift in order to plan again must not lose a colleague's request (spec §2: requests are taken as musts). Cost if wrong: one dispatch in `MonthScreen.onChoose` and one test.
- R15: W3's label on the `Pravidla` screen becomes `Nejvýš 5 dní v kuse, pak 2 dny volna` (At most 5 days in a row, then 2 days off); it was `Po 5–6 dnech 2 dny volna` (After 5–6 days 2 days off). Why: since R6, W3 charges runs over 5 up to the maximum, a lone day off, and one day off after 5 or more; two days off after 6 is the must M10, and the old label described the must and not the wish. Cost if wrong: one label.
- R16: Strong-wish lines in `Kontrola` keep the attribute `data-strong` and the 8 px bar, though a reviewer called them extra. Why: wish lines were amber already, so without a mark the strong tier, which the brief asks `Kontrola` to set apart, would not show. Cost if wrong: one attribute and one CSS rule.
- R17: `NIGHTS_CHOICES.extra`, `Rád budu brát noční směny i za mé kolegy, pokud je to možné.` (I will gladly take nights for my colleagues, if possible), stays as it is, and so do the domain's `nerad(a)` and `rádi` forms. Why: the Phase 1 spec ties that option to the sentence of the team's survey, so that the team lead can copy the answers over; the rule of gender-neutral wording (spec §3.9) covers text addressed to the user, which a colleague's survey answer is not; the `(a)` forms are inclusive already. Cost if wrong: one line and one test constant.
- R18: Task 8's judgment calls are accepted: the order of the seven numbers on the `Pravidla` screen by subject, with the time budget last; the limits of a person in a grid of their own; `generalMaxTwelve` as a prop; the `badInput` guard with a write on blur, which tells "no limit" from "a typo erased the limit" (a maximum of 0 nights is a HARD must, so a silent erase would let the generator give nights); no ruling numbers in comments in the source; the W3 label tested in `text.test.ts`; the question before a person is removed tested for its mention of the notes. Cost if wrong: an order of fields, or a write on blur against one on change.
- R19: `parseDoctor` accepts optional whitespace around the dash and after the `L`; hours 0–23 with or without a leading zero; minutes of exactly two digits, 00–59; and `24` or `24:00` as the end only (1440 minutes, which a note allows). Anything else gives null, a bare `L` and a start not before the end included, and the cell then goes the existing way of an unrecognised value. Why: the team lead writes `L` with the time ("píšeme i čas": we write the time too), and a wrong guess must not store an appointment that nobody made. Cost if wrong: one regular expression and its tests.
- R20: `personRowTsv` copies a cell's code whenever the cell has one (a shift, `x` or an absence), and the note only on a day with no cell. This is the spec over the brief. Why: spec §3.5 says "a day with only an `L` note copies `L`"; the brief's looser "a day without a shift but with a note" would lose `DOV`, `NEM` and `x` from the copy, and the sheet in Excel must show them. Cost if wrong: one condition and the test of five codes.
- R21: `parseDoctor` also accepts a dot between hours and minutes (`L 8.30-12`), the usual Czech way of writing a time. This amends R19. Minutes stay exactly two digits, so `L 8.5-12` and `8h` stay unrecognised. Cost if wrong: one character class and two tests.
- R22: Task 9's other calls are accepted: a paste of a note mirrors the skip rule of the code (it writes on days of the tail and outside a person's active period, as a code does); `Použít` (Apply) counts notes; a note in the first column marks it as a day column; `applyPaste` trusts `planPaste`, its only producer, and a round trip through the parser is tested. The ledger names no cost: three of the four follow how a code already behaves in the same paste, and the fourth rests on `planPaste` being the only producer of the plan.
- R23: `plan-quarter.spec.ts` plans each month with 10 s instead of the brief's 5 s, and keeps `Musí: 0` for every month. Why: the 5 s was copied from `plan-january.spec.ts` as a shortcut for CI; February and March (a tail, the Easter holidays) get too few iterations at 5 s since Task 6's drop of 41 % per iteration (one person one weekday shift short in 8–26 % of quarters, more under load). Cost if wrong: about 15 s more per run of the e2e specs, and budgets below 10 s are not covered; so the README tells the team lead to generate again, or to raise `Čas na generování (s)`, when a month ends below FPD. The premise did not hold: at 10 s about 7.5 % of the quarters still failed. R24 superseded this, and the spec is back at 5 s.
- R24: Task 6b is added: a deterministic repair of the hours in the generator. After a segment, for each person below FPD as M6 judges it, it adds one shift directly into spare coverage, or by a chain of two people, and accepts only changes that lower the score and break no HARD must, repeating while it helps. Why: spec §3.8 lets M6 go short only when it is forced; March 2027 is tight by construction (Easter leaves 21 regular weekdays against an FPD of 23 × 7,5 h) and the remaining shortfall of one shift was believed to be a two-step valley that the annealing does not cross (R25 found its true shape). Acceptance: at least 98 % of quarters clean over at least 50 at 5 s in the browser; no scenario below its rate over seeds 1–24; iterations per second within 5 %; the spec back to 5 s if that holds. Cost if wrong: one task, and a repair that could hide a problem of the search (guarded by tests and sweeps).
- R25: The trade step is approved. Before the sets and chains of the brief, the short person A may make one trade that keeps A's number of shifts: a `swapDay` on a day both work, a balanced `swapRange` of 2–7 days, an `exchange`, or a `shiftMove` of A's own shift; each step is filtered by `breaksHardMust` and undone by its inverse. Why: A's row was packed (every free day breaks M9, M10 or M5), so sets and chains fixed 0 of 44 broken months; with trades alone 40 of 44, and 200 of 200 in Node and 50 of 50 in the browser at 5 s together with R26; iterations per second, the sweep and the wish scores were unchanged or better. Added requirements (R25 to R27): no candidate may change a fixed cell (new test); an exact undo (rows and score identical after a pass that changes nothing) and a guaranteed end (a real gain, not rounding noise); the time of the repair reported on every scenario month without a budget; the case in `search.test.ts` stays fast. Cost if wrong: `repair.ts` is larger than planned and takes up to about 200 ms a month; one commit to revert.
- R26: The repair runs at the end of each segment and on the early-return path. Why: 200 of 200 against 196 of 200 in Node, and 50 of 50 against 48 of 50 in the browser, for a repair at the very end only, which also costs about 430 more in wish penalty on the Marches that it fixes. Cost if wrong: the trajectories of searches without a budget differ from before (the sweep found the same pass rates: 24 of 24 everywhere, `doctor` 19 of 24 on the same seeds).
- R27: A time cap applies in budget mode only, as a stop that `search` passes to `repairHours(st, stop?)`: no new trade once the elapsed time is at least the budget plus 200 ms. Without a budget there is no cap and no clock. Why: the worst repair measured was 185 ms a month under load, and the team lead's laptop may be slower. Cost if wrong: a repair that is capped leaves what the annealing found, which is the old behaviour. R30 amends the cap.
- R28: The repair runs after each segment that ran at least one iteration. Why: it keeps the test "returns its first draft when the time budget is already spent" as it is written, and a segment with no iteration found nothing new. Cost if wrong: a run whose first draft eats the whole budget returns the draft unrepaired.
- R29: On a stop, the pass of the repair that is in hand is dropped, and a test pins it. Why: it is the literal reading of the requirement that a stop ends the repair at once, and the stop fires only at the budget plus 200 ms. Cost if wrong: in budget mode, a change that was found is rarely lost.
- R30: The repair on the early-return path gets `stopAfter(started, Math.min(budget, elapsedMs) + 200 ms)`, so that a `Zastavit` (Stop) gives it 200 ms from the stop, and the return when time runs out is unchanged. This amends R27. Why: a stop by the user was capped only at the budget plus 200 ms, so at the moment of the stop it was not capped at all (the reviewer measured at most 23.9 ms on 108 stopped searches, so the risk was latent); tying the stop to the user's stop would, with R29, skip the repair of every stop. Cost if wrong: the repair of a stopped run is cut at 200 ms.
- R31: M12 judges a person against the smaller of `settings.minWeekendShifts` and the person's effective maximum of 12-hour shifts, and quotes that value in `(min. N)`. Why: the HARD maximum wins over a minimum that may be left short when it is forced (spec §3.8); a cap of 0 or 1 (a colleague who cannot work 12-hour shifts) otherwise gave an M12 line every month that nothing could clear, and so taught the team lead to read past `Musí: N` (Musts: N). Cost if wrong: a person capped below the minimum is never flagged for too few weekends; the team lead set the cap knowingly.
- R32: `parseSaveFile` drops the doctor's notes of a person who is not in `people` and still refuses a malformed note, whoever it belongs to. This amends R2, as R2 foresaw. Why: refusing the whole file (`Soubor je poškozený.`, The file is damaged.) is the costliest answer to a harmless inconsistency of a hand edit, and the cells of unknown people are accepted already. Cost if wrong: a hand-edited orphan note disappears silently.
- R33: The three per-month labels are unified on "za měsíc", the wording Phase 1 shows: `Max. 12h směn za měsíc` on the card, `Max. nočních za měsíc` and `Min. víkendových směn za měsíc` (they read "měsíčně"). Why: one screen mixed the two wordings. Cost if wrong: three labels and their tests.
- R34: `q1-2027` asserts `Musí: 0` (no must unit) for every month only if a sweep over seeds 1–24 at the scenario's own number of iterations is 24 of 24; otherwise the rate is reported and the assertion left out. Why: the final reviewer found 24 of 24 months clean at 600,000 iterations on seeds 1–8, but the scenario's count may differ. Cost if wrong: M6 and M12 stay unpinned for a realistic quarter. The sweep over seeds 1–24 at 300,000 iterations was 24 of 24 (72 months with no must unit), so the assertion is in; the other scenarios gave the counts they gave before the fix wave.
- R35: The ten points that the final review declined to judge all stay declined, as it reasoned. They are: M12 for people who cannot reach the minimum (a month of leave); a holiday pin that is always a claim; a raised personal maximum of 12-hour shifts that allows extra weekends but does not produce them; settings of a hand-edited file that are not type-checked; STRONG that beats the gains of one night and not every combination; a boundary horizon of one day into the next month; W10 collisions without a line in `Kontrola`; no screen for the running counts or for dropping a month's record (Phase 2); a quarter's 12-hour spread of 2 (R12); and a Phase 1 and a Phase 1.1 build that share the cache key `shift-scheduler/v1`. The first three go to the team lead as questions, and the second also gets a line in the README; the last goes to the user as "use only the newest build file". Cost if wrong: those named in the questions below.
- R36: The final fix wave takes the one Important finding and the seven Minor ones of the final review, every parked minor marked as a candidate for the wave by Tasks 6 to 10 and 6b (R30 among them), two cheap guards on HARD rules (the negative `restBefore` of Task 3 and the claimed N12 as a night of Task 4), and the docs pass with this file. The other parked minors (refactors, file splits, comments, nits of speed) stay open and are listed below. Why: one fix dispatch, kept to defects, guards and docs. Cost if wrong: the refactors wait for Phase 2.
- R37: In a paste, a first column that holds only an `L` and words (`L dopoledne`, L morning) is still taken for names: only an `L` followed by a digit counts as a day value there. Why: the team lead writes notes with the time (R19), the paste dialog says that the first column is left out, and a broader rule (an `L` and a space) would take a name written with an initial for a day. Cost if wrong: such a paste shifts by one day, with the dialog's warning shown.
- R38: The fix wave's extras are accepted: `isBelowFpd` is also used by the first draft (`construct.ts`) and the totals (`Grid.tsx`), and `FPD_TOLERANCE` is private; the e2e click on another cell presses the 18th cell after a hit test, since the 25th lies under the totals at 1280 × 720; R30 and the skipped repair are pinned by tests behind a call-through `vi.mock('./repair')` in `search.test.ts`. Why: each follows the brief's intent and is tested. Cost if wrong: two hunks to revert.

## After Phase 1.1: version 1.1.1

The user's decisions of 9 Nov 2026, after trying 1.1.0. A save into a new file, meant as a backup, made the page go on saving into that new file.

- The save bar has three buttons: `Uložit` (Save), `Uložit jako…` (Save as…) and `Otevřít soubor` (Open file). `Uložit jako…` works as in other programs: the page goes on saving into the new file, and the file before stays as it was. The user chose this over a "save a copy" that would keep saving into the old file.
- The page always opens at the start screen, `Otevřít soubor` or `Začít nový`. The browser keeps no copy of the plan anymore, so nothing is restored after a reload, and the user declined a "restore unsaved changes" choice. The warning before closing a page with changes that are not in a file stays. What an older version left in the browser's storage is neither read nor removed.
- GitHub Actions builds and tests every push, and a version tag publishes a release with the built file (`.github/workflows/build.yml`).

## Questions for the team lead

- Should M12 be pro-rated for people on long leave? Today a person whose absences, `x` requests or dates in the team make 2 weekend shifts impossible is flagged in red, as spec §3.4 asks.
- Is "a holiday pin is always a claim" still right? A pin made only to keep a generated holiday shift on the next run also takes that shift out of the counts of M5 and M12 (the figure `3+1` and its title show it).
- Do the colleagues who offered more weekends need a per-person target? A raised maximum of 12-hour shifts allows more but does not produce them: W5a's target and W7 pull the person back to the team's count.
- W7 lets the 12-hour shifts of a quarter differ by 2 between people on most seeds (R12). Is that fair enough, or should W7 weigh more across the quarter?
- How fast is the laptop the plan will be made on, against the 20 s default of `Čas na generování (s)` (Time to generate, s)? The default was checked on a fast desktop (20 of 20 quarters clean at 20 s); no test covers a laptop that is 2–4 times slower.
